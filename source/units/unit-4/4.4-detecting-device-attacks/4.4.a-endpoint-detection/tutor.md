# 4.4.a tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
The reframe to make before this lecture starts: a firewall is a guard standing at the front door, and EDR is a guard who already lives inside the building, the moment malicious code is actually running on a device, a boundary control can no longer see it, because the code isn't crossing the boundary anymore, it's already past it. Everything interesting about EDR follows from that one positional difference: it has to watch behavior happening *on* the machine, in real time, which is a fundamentally different job than inspecting traffic as it crosses a perimeter.

## The big picture
The Meridian scenario, Office spawning PowerShell, PowerShell making an unusual outbound connection, a new executable landing in a temp folder, is worth remembering as a worked example of "living off the land," and it's the cleanest illustration in this unit of why signature-based defense (4.3.b) structurally cannot catch everything: PowerShell itself is completely legitimate software that ships with Windows, so no signature will ever flag it, and the only way to catch this attack chain is to notice the *sequence* is abnormal even though every individual step used trusted tools. That's the same behavioral-baseline logic from endpoint protection, just extended across four different telemetry streams, process, file, registry, network, instead of one, and it directly sets up the next two atoms: EDR gives you a live, single-device view, log analysis (4.4.b) extends that same pattern-recognition instinct across many systems and across time, and indicators of compromise (4.4.c) formalizes how you combine clues from both into a confident verdict. Keep the response half in mind too, collect-detect-respond in one agent is specifically what separates EDR from a SIEM (which correlates but can't act) and from antivirus (which detects but rarely isolates), and isolation is worth remembering precisely: it cuts network access to stop lateral movement, it does not wipe or destroy anything.
