# 2.1.c tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
Hold onto this one plain fact: risk is not the same thing as threat, and the difference is the entire point of this skill. A threat is the bad thing that could happen. Risk is how much you should actually worry about it, likelihood times impact, a number you can compare against other numbers. That multiplication is what turns security from a vague sense of dread into something you can put a budget against, because a security team that tries to defend against every threat equally is a security team that has already lost the argument for resources.

## The big picture
The four-treatment vocabulary, mitigate, accept, transfer, avoid, is worth memorizing cold, because it's the AP exam's cleanest test of whether you can reason about a scenario rather than just recite a definition. The trap most students fall into is assuming "mitigate" is always the right answer, since it sounds the most responsible. It isn't. Spending $20,000 to protect a $10,000 asset is a worse decision than accepting the risk outright, and buying insurance (transfer) is often smarter than trying to engineer your way to zero risk. The organizations that manage risk well aren't the ones that eliminate it, they're the ones that spend their limited defense budget on the risks where a dollar of mitigation actually buys a dollar (or more) of reduced expected loss.
