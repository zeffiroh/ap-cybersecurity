# 2.1.b tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
The single habit this skill is trying to build: before you design a defense, name the attacker. "Someone might attack us" is not a threat model, "a script kiddie scanning for unpatched software" and "a nation-state after our research data" are, and they lead to opposite spending decisions. A patch cycle stops the first and barely slows the second. Get in the habit of asking who and why before you ask how, because the six categories in this skill aren't trivia to memorize, they're a sorting mechanism that tells you where your budget actually belongs.

## The big picture
Meridian University's Monday morning is a small case study in a big idea: the same organization can be targeted by wildly different actors in the same 24 hours, and treating them identically wastes the response. A ransomware note calls for law enforcement and offline backups. A public data leak calls for an access-log audit aimed at insiders. A defaced website calls for credential hygiene and a PR plan. Notice, too, that APT isn't a seventh category, it's a description of *style* (patient, stealthy, custom-tooled) that mostly, but not always, points back to a nation-state actor. Keep that distinction sharp; it's one of the AP exam's favorite places to test whether you actually understand the categories or just memorized their names.
