# 2.3.b tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
The framing to hold onto: a camera is a detective control wearing a preventive control's reputation. People install CCTV and feel safer, but a camera has never once physically stopped anyone from doing anything, it only makes it more likely they'll be identified afterward, and the deterrent effect only works if the attacker believes that matters to them. Keep that distinction sharp, because the AP exam loves testing whether students can tell a preventive control from a detective one, and "we have cameras" is one of the most common wrong answers to "how did you prevent this."

## The big picture
The analog-versus-IP tradeoff in this skill is a small, concrete example of a huge pattern in modern security: every time you add intelligence and connectivity to a device, you also add an attack surface that didn't exist before. An analog coaxial camera can be physically cut but can't be hacked over the internet; an IP camera can be watched from anywhere in the world by its owner, and, if the default password was never changed, by anyone else too. That tradeoff (capability versus attack surface) isn't unique to cameras. You'll meet it again with every "smart" device in this course, and the right response is never "don't use the smart version," it's "secure the smart version as seriously as you'd secure a server, because that's what it now is."
