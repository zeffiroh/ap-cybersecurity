# 2.3.c tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
The idea to internalize here is that layers don't add security, they *multiply* the attacker's cost, and that distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. If a facility has five independent control categories, an attacker doesn't need to be five times as capable; they need to succeed at all five simultaneously, and failing any single one ends the attempt. That's a fundamentally different kind of hard than "climb a slightly taller fence." It's also why the depth score in this skill counts *categories*, not raw control count, three guards covering the same job as one guard adds cost without closing a single new gap.

## The big picture
Notice what a guard can do that nothing else in this unit can: reason. A badge reader logs a swipe; a guard notices that the person swiping doesn't match their badge photo, or that their explanation for being in the building doesn't add up. That's the throughline connecting this skill back to social engineering in Unit 1, technology is consistent and tireless but has no judgment, and humans have judgment but get tired and make exceptions. A mature security posture doesn't pick one over the other; it deliberately pairs them so each one covers the other's blind spot. That pairing principle is worth remembering every time this course asks you to evaluate whether a security plan is "strong, adequate, or weak."
