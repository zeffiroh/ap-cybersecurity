# 1.4.a tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
The unsettling reframe this skill asks you to make: for your entire life, hearing a familiar voice or seeing a familiar face has been *evidence* of identity, strong enough evidence that we built our entire social trust system on it. That evidence just became forgeable at consumer-hardware cost. The professor's intuition here is to stop asking "does this sound/look real to me?" (a losing arms race against improving models) and start asking "does this interaction include something a clone *couldn't* have access to?", a shared secret the model was never trained on, a callback to a number you already had.

## The big picture
The phrase "double-stack" in this skill's closing paragraph is worth remembering as a template you'll reapply constantly: a cloned voice defeats your *recognition* heuristic, and manufactured urgency defeats your *verification* heuristic, and they're deployed together specifically because each one is disarming a different defense. Whenever an attack combines a trust exploit with a time-pressure exploit, assume it's not an accident, it's the attacker covering both of your natural safety checks at once.
