# 1.1.a tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
Here's the mental model worth keeping for the whole unit: every social-engineering message is an attempt to make you skip your own internal verification step. Urgency skips it by racing the clock. Authority skips it by outsourcing your judgment to someone else's. Scarcity skips it by turning verification into a cost ("if I check, I'll miss the deal"). Intimidation skips it by making *not* complying feel dangerous. Once you see all four as variations on one move, disable your slow, careful thinking, you stop needing to memorize scam templates, because the templates change every year but the move never does.

## The big picture
Notice that not one of these four tactics required the attacker to know anything about computers. That's the whole point of starting the course here: before you learn to harden a server or read a packet capture, you need to internalize that the cheapest, most reliable attack surface in any organization is a human being's nervous system under pressure. Every technical control you'll learn for the rest of this course, MFA, segmentation, detection rules, exists downstream of an attacker successfully doing what we practiced today. Getting this instinct right early pays off in every unit that follows.
