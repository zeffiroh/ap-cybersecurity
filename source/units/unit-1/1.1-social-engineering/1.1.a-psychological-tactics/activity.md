# In-class activity, 1.1.a Psychological tactics

**Format:** pairs · **Time:** 20 min · **Offline:** yes

## Goal
Students learn to name the psychological tactic(s) behind a message and justify a defensive
response. Ties to LO 1.1.A (identify indicators) and 1.1.B (explain how tactics influence victims).

## Materials (all local)
- the SimLab simulation on each machine, or the printed message set below.

## Steps
1. Each pair opens the simulation and works through the message stream, agreeing on the tactic tag(s)
   for each before submitting. They must reach the target score.
2. For any message they got wrong, they write one sentence explaining *why* the tactic works
   psychologically (what feeling it engineers).
3. Pairs draft one original message of their own that stacks at least two tactics, then trade with
   another pair to classify.

## Collaborate (AP skill 4)
Assign roles: one partner is the "attacker" (defends why their crafted message is convincing), the
other is the "analyst" (names the tactics and the defense). Swap roles for the second message.

## Exit ticket
Each student submits: one real-feeling message they wrote + the tactics it uses + the correct
defensive action.

## Instructor notes
- Expected tactics map to the four labels: urgency, authority, scarcity, intimidation.
- Common stumble: students conflate scarcity ("only 3 left") with urgency ("act in 1 hour"). Clarify
  scarcity = limited *supply*, urgency = limited *time*.
- Feeds the unit project (phishing analyzer) and scenario stage 1 (social-engineering entry).
