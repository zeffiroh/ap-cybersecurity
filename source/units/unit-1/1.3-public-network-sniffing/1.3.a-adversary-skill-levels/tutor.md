# 1.3.a tutor notes

Notes for the pre- and post-lecture tutor posts.

## The intuition to hold onto
Resist the instinct to imagine every attacker as a hooded figure in a dark room running custom exploits. The honest picture is a long-tailed distribution: an enormous number of low-skilled opportunists running free tools against anyone in range, and a small number of well-resourced, patient operators aiming precisely at specific high-value targets. The threat you actually face on a given afternoon at a coffee shop is overwhelmingly the first kind, which is good news, because the defenses against low-skilled, high-volume attacks (a VPN, basic skepticism, avoiding open networks) are cheap and effective, unlike defending against a funded zero-day operator.

## The big picture
Pairing skill tier with motivation is what turns this from trivia into a genuinely useful risk-assessment tool: the *same* observable behavior (someone scanning nearby Wi-Fi networks) means something completely different depending on whether the actor wants your credit card, your company's trade secrets, or just the thrill of proving they can do it. Every later topic in this unit, evil twins, jamming, war driving, is easier to reason about once you've internalized that the attacker's *goal* predicts their *method*, and the method predicts what evidence they'll leave behind.
