Lab, Finding the Attack in the Logs

Triage a real auth log with grep-style search, find the attacker, and prove the breach.

Below is an authentication log from an SSH server, the kind an analyst reviews after an alert. One source address ran a password-guessing campaign against the server, and one of those guesses eventually worked. The log is noisy with normal traffic, so you will not spot the attack by reading top to bottom. Instead you will filter it the way a real analyst does, with search, exactly as grep would on the command line. Your search box accepts plain text or a regular expression.

What the triage proved

No single log line says "attack." The signal is in the shape of the set: one source address producing a dense run of authentication failures, followed by one success. Reading the whole log is impractical, so analysts filter it, grouping by source and by outcome until the pattern stands out. That is why LO 4.4.B stresses correlating multiple entries over time rather than trusting any one event, and why centralized, searchable logs are a core detection control. The success line is the moment the account fell; in a real incident it marks the start of the response: isolate that host and reset that credential.
