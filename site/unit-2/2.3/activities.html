Physical Access Controls · 4

Skill 2.3.a · pairs · 20 min · Offline: yes

Physical access controls (locks, badges, biometrics, mantraps)

Goal

Each pair produces a completed facility audit worksheet: a table listing which controls are present or absent at a fictional facility, the gaps those absences create, and a ranked recommendation list of the two most important controls to add first. This ties directly to LO 2.3.A (explain how physical access controls mitigate risk).

Materials

Printed handout (cut here or project on screen)

Facility audit worksheet

Facility name (fictional): Northgate Data Center, Building C, Server Floor

Scenario description: The server floor houses 12 racks of equipment for a fictional regional bank. The floor is accessed from a lobby on the ground level. A receptionist sits in the lobby during business hours only (8 a.m. to 6 p.m., weekdays). The building has a perimeter fence with a gate that is unlocked during business hours.

Control inventory (circle one for each row):

Control Present? Notes
Keyed or cipher lock on server-floor door Yes / No
Badge/RFID reader on server-floor door Yes / No
Biometric reader inside server-floor entrance Yes / No
Mantrap between lobby and server floor Yes / No
CCTV cameras covering the server floor entrance Yes / No
Security guard present 24/7 Yes / No

Step 1, Scenario A (5 min): Your instructor reads the following configuration aloud. Circle Yes or No for each control above.

Scenario A: The server-floor door has a keyed deadbolt. There is a badge reader mounted beside the door. There is no mantrap. A fixed CCTV camera covers the hallway outside the door. There is no dedicated security guard; the receptionist monitors a lobby camera during business hours only. There is no biometric reader.

Step 2, Gap analysis (5 min): Using the rules below, list every gap present in Scenario A.

Gap rules (apply ALL that match): - If keyed lock is absent AND badge reader is absent: gap "no_key_control" - If badge reader is absent AND biometric is absent: gap "no_electronic_access" - If mantrap is absent: gap "no_tailgating_prevention" - If CCTV is absent: gap "no_visual_monitoring" - If security guard is absent AND biometric is absent: gap "no_identity_verification"

Gaps found in Scenario A (list them):

Step 3, Prioritize (5 min): Rank the gaps from most critical to least critical for this bank scenario. Write a one-sentence justification for your top pick.

Most critical gap: ___________

Justification: ___________

Second most critical gap: ___________

Step 4, Scenario B (5 min): Your instructor reads a second configuration. Repeat the gap analysis.

Scenario B: No keyed lock. No badge reader. No biometric. No mantrap. CCTV is present. One licensed security guard is stationed at the door 24/7.

Gaps found in Scenario B:

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (2 min) One partner opens the simulation and quickly reads the objective panel so both partners understand the audit model. The other partner reads the gap rules in problem/prompt.md.
  2. (5 min) Together, complete Step 1 and Step 2 on the handout for Scenario A.
  3. (5 min) Complete Step 3: agree on a priority ranking. If you disagree, each writes a one-sentence argument and you pick the stronger one.
  4. (5 min) Complete Step 4 for Scenario B independently (each partner fills in their own sheet), then compare answers.
  5. (3 min) Prepare a one-sentence summary to share with the class: "The most dangerous gap in Scenario A/B is ___ because ___."

Collaborate

Assign roles before starting: one partner is the Security Auditor (reads controls off the scenario and checks Yes/No), the other is the Risk Analyst (applies the gap rules and writes the gap list). For Step 3, switch roles so the Risk Analyst argues for the priority ranking and the Security Auditor challenges it. Both names go on the sheet.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits (on a half-sheet or typed into the class portal): "Name one physical control that prevents tailgating and explain in one sentence why a badge reader alone cannot solve the tailgating problem."

Expected answer: A mantrap (access vestibule) prevents tailgating by interlocking two doors so only one person can pass through per cycle. A badge reader authenticates the person who presents the badge but cannot physically stop a second person from following them through the door before it closes.

Skill 2.3.b · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Surveillance systems (CCTV, monitoring)

Goal

Groups act as a physical-security consultant team reviewing a proposed CCTV camera layout for a fictional office floor plan, identify blind spots and monitoring/logical weaknesses, and propose fixes. Ties to LO 2.3.A (surveillance systems) and the course skills Mitigate Risk and Detect Attacks.

Materials

Camera layout sheet (print or project)

Floor plan (top-down, ASCII): the building has one main entrance (west), a loading dock (south), a server room (northeast corner), and a long back hallway (east edge).

# Camera Location Type Coverage note Blind spot / issue? Fix
1 CAM-01 Main entrance (west) Fixed Faces the door, well-lit lobby ? ?
2 CAM-02 Loading dock (south) Fixed Points at the dock doors only ? ?
3 CAM-03 Server room (NE) Fixed Mounted 6 ft high, admin creds still default admin/admin ? ?
4 CAM-04 Back hallway (east edge) PTZ, sweeps hallway every 45s Currently pointed north when guard checked ? ?
5 (none) Loading dock side door — No camera covers the side door next to the dock ? ?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each group reviews the floor plan and camera list individually and marks which rows describe a blind spot, a logical/credential vulnerability, or both.
  2. (5 min) Group discusses row 3 (server room camera): why is a low mounting height plus default credentials a compounding risk, not just two separate minor issues? Fill in "issue" and "fix."
  3. (5 min) Repeat for row 4 (PTZ hallway camera), explain in the group's own words why a PTZ camera's wide average coverage does not guarantee coverage at any single instant, and what an intruder who studies the sweep timing could do.
  4. (5 min) Discuss row 5, a completely uncovered door. Propose where a new camera (or an overlapping field of view from an existing one) should go to close the gap.
  5. (5 min) Each group opens the simulation and checks their blind-spot / vulnerability calls against the sim's scoring for the analogous scenarios; groups revise any incorrect calls.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one member is the "site surveyor" (identifies physical blind spots from the floor plan), one is the "network auditor" (flags logical/credential weaknesses on IP cameras), and remaining members are "budget owners" (must justify each proposed fix, new camera, re-aim, credential change, VLAN isolation, against its cost and the risk it reduces). For row 4, the group must specifically debate whether the fix is a second camera, a different sweep pattern, or switching to a fixed camera.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes one sentence: "Camera/location X has [blind spot / logical vulnerability] because [reasoning tied to the lesson], and [fix] would close it by [mechanism]."

Skill 2.3.c · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Security guards and layered defense-in-depth

Goal

Groups act as physical security consultants reviewing layer proposals for a fictional facility, Novara Data Center, and decide whether each proposed layer set is STRONG, ADEQUATE, or WEAK, and what single addition would most improve a weak configuration. Ties to LO 2.3.B (security guards and layered defense-in-depth) and the course skill Mitigate Risk.

Materials

Layer sheet (print or project)

# Facility Layers present Categories Rating What's missing
1 Novara-Annex lobby guard, roving night patrol ? ? ?
2 Novara-Branch perimeter fence, badge reader, CCTV ? ? ?
3 Novara-Warehouse unlocked side door only ? ? ?
4 Novara-Lab 3 separate CCTV feeds ? ? ?
5 Novara-Core fence, guard post, badge reader, mantrap, CCTV ? ? ?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each group reads rows 1-2 and individually lists the distinct control categories present for each (perimeter, personnel, electronic, barrier, monitoring).
  2. (5 min) Group discusses: row 1 has two layers but why does that not raise the depth score above 1? Reach consensus on the "categories," "rating," and "what's missing" columns for rows 1-2.
  3. (5 min) Repeat for rows 3-4, paying special attention to why three CCTV feeds (row 4) still count as only one category, and why row 3 has a depth score of zero rather than one.
  4. (5 min) For row 5, the group must decide whether this facility is STRONG or ADEQUATE, and justify the answer by counting distinct categories, not layers.
  5. (5 min) Each group opens the simulation and checks their ratings against the sim's scoring for the analogous facilities; groups revise any incorrect calls and note which category was missing in each WEAK or ADEQUATE case.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one member is the "facility manager" (proposes which single new layer would most cheaply raise the depth score), one is the "budget owner" (must justify that the proposed layer's cost is worth the new category it closes, not a redundant one), and remaining members are "auditors" (challenge whether a proposed addition actually introduces a new category or just duplicates an existing one, e.g., a second guard vs. a badge reader). For row 1 (Novara-Annex), the group must specifically debate whether adding a third guard or adding a badge reader is the better use of budget, and explain why in terms of depth score.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes one sentence: "Facility X is rated [STRONG/ADEQUATE/WEAK] because it has [N] distinct control categories, and adding a [category] layer such as [example] would most improve its posture."

Skill 2.3.d · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Environmental controls (HVAC, fire suppression, power)

Goal

Groups act as facility-design consultants who must specify environmental controls for a new three-room data center build (server room, network/telecom closet, and general office), and justify every control choice against the hazard and equipment it protects. Ties to LO 2.3.C (mitigate risk to physical spaces) and the course skill Mitigate Risk.

Materials

Design sheet (print or project)

# Room Hazard to mitigate Candidate controls Chosen control Justification
1 server-room Fire water sprinkler / clean-agent (FM-200) / no suppression ? ?
2 server-room Total utility power loss UPS only / UPS + generator / generator only ? ?
3 server-room Hot/cold air mixing raising inlet temp containment curtains / lower ceiling / more fans ? ?
4 network-closet Single point of failure in cooling single HVAC unit / N+1 redundant HVAC units ? ?
5 server-room Undetected floor-level leak no sensor / raised floor + water sensor ? ?
6 office Fire water sprinkler / clean-agent (FM-200) ? ?
7 server-room Locked-up server requiring reboot send technician in person / managed PDU remote cycle ? ?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each group reads rows 1-3 (server room fire, power, and airflow) and individually picks a control for each, based on the atom.md lesson.
  2. (5 min) Group discusses: why is a water sprinkler wrong for row 1 but acceptable for row 6? Reach consensus and fill in "justification" for rows 1, 3, 6.
  3. (5 min) Repeat for rows 2, 4, 5, 7, the power, redundancy, leak-detection, and remote-access controls. Debate the UPS-vs-generator tradeoff for row 2 explicitly (speed of response vs. runtime).
  4. (5 min) Each group opens the simulation and checks their control choices against the sim's scoring for the analogous scenarios; groups revise any incorrect picks.
  5. (5 min) Report-out: each group states one control they initially picked wrong and what detail in the scenario changed their mind.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one member is the "facilities lead" (owns the final control decision for each row), one is the "budget owner" (must justify the added cost of redundancy, N+1 generators, managed PDUs, against the outage risk it reduces), and remaining members are "inspectors" (challenge whether the chosen control actually matches the room's contents and hazard). For row 4 (single HVAC unit), the group must specifically debate what N+1 redundancy means and why a single non-redundant HVAC unit is a design flaw even if it currently works.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes one sentence: "For room [X] and hazard [Y], the correct control is [Z] because [reasoning tied to the equipment at risk or the redundancy principle], and choosing [wrong alternative] would fail because [reason]."
