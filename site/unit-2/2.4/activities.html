2.4 Activities

Physical Detection Systems · 3

Skill 2.4.a · pairs · 25 min · Offline: yes

Access logs and monitoring

Goal

Students practice reading physical access log data to identify anomalous patterns (after-hours access, repeated denial bursts, location inconsistencies) and articulate what each pattern indicates to a security analyst. Ties to LO 2.4.A (analyze access logs to detect physical attack indicators).

Materials

Printed log extract (use if machines are unavailable)

TIMESTAMP            BADGE    DOOR              OUTCOME
2024-03-12 07:58:02  B-0101   lobby-north       GRANTED
2024-03-12 08:03:14  B-0205   lobby-north       GRANTED
2024-03-12 08:45:30  B-0101   server-room-A     GRANTED
2024-03-12 12:10:05  B-0307   lobby-north       DENIED
2024-03-12 12:10:18  B-0307   lobby-north       DENIED
2024-03-12 12:10:29  B-0307   lobby-north       DENIED
2024-03-12 12:10:41  B-0307   lobby-north       DENIED
2024-03-12 12:10:55  B-0307   lobby-north       DENIED
2024-03-12 12:11:03  B-0307   lobby-north       DENIED
2024-03-12 17:55:00  B-0205   lobby-north       GRANTED
2024-03-12 23:41:17  B-0205   server-room-A     GRANTED
2024-03-13 00:05:44  B-0101   datacenter-offsite GRANTED

(Note: datacenter-offsite is 35 miles from the main building. B-0101 was last seen at server-room-A at 08:45.)

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each pair reads through the log. For every row, decide: normal or anomalous? Write a one-word verdict in the margin.
  2. For each anomalous row, write which rule it triggers: AFTER-HOURS, BURST, or LOCATION-INCONSISTENCY.
  3. Discuss: which anomaly is the most serious, and what would you recommend the analyst do next?

Collaborate

Assign roles: one partner is the "analyst" (flags entries and names the rule), the other is the "attacker's lawyer" (argues why the entry might be innocent). After classifying all entries, swap roles and revisit the two most disputed entries.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: (1) which badge IDs you would report to security, (2) the rule that triggered each flag, and (3) one defensive countermeasure that would reduce the risk each flagged pattern represents.

Skill 2.4.b · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Alarm systems

Goal

Groups act as an alarm-monitoring central station reviewing a batch of sensor events for a small office, decide the correct classification and response tier for each, and design one improvement to reduce false alarms. Ties to LO 2.4.B (alarm systems) and the course skill Detect Attacks.

Materials

Event log (print or project)

# Zone Sensor Arm state Seconds since armed Classification? Response?
1 front-lobby contact ARMED_AWAY 8 ? ?
2 file-room contact DISARMED 0 ? ?
3 loading-dock glass_break ARMED_AWAY 300 ? ?
4 reception-desk panic ARMED_STAY 0 ? ?
5 rear-hallway contact ARMED_AWAY 310 ? ?
6 side-door contact ARMED_AWAY 44 ? ?

(Rows 3 and 5 occur 10 seconds apart in the same batch, decide whether that matters.)

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each group reads rows 1-2 individually and decides classification (SUPPRESSED, FALSE_ALARM, LOCAL_ONLY, or DISPATCH) using the exit-delay and arm-state rules from the lesson.
  2. (5 min) Group discusses row 4 (panic button): does arm state matter here at all? Why does this sensor type break the normal rules?
  3. (7 min) Group works rows 3 and 5 together: both are real trips (past exit delay) in different zones only 10 seconds apart. Decide whether cross-zone verification applies and what the combined classification should be, then classify row 6 (boundary case) on its own.
  4. (5 min) Each group opens the simulation and checks their calls against the sim's scoring for analogous scenarios; groups revise any incorrect calls.
  5. (3 min) Report-out: each group states which row was hardest to agree on and why.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one member is the "panel operator" (makes the classification call in real time), one is the "dispatcher" (decides, given a classification, whether police get called), and remaining members are "auditors" (challenge whether the exit-delay or verification rule was applied correctly). For row 6 (44 seconds, one second inside the 45-second boundary), the group must specifically debate what happens if the exit-delay constant were shortened, and what tradeoff that creates between response speed and false-alarm rate.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes one sentence: "Row X should be classified as [SUPPRESSED / FALSE_ALARM / LOCAL_ONLY / DISPATCH] because [reasoning tied to arm state, exit delay, or cross-zone verification]."

Skill 2.4.c · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Motion detectors and sensor-based detection

Goal

Groups act as a physical-security design team laying out motion-sensor coverage for a small server room, choosing sensor technology per zone and tuning sensitivity to balance detection against false-positive risk. Ties to LO 2.4.B/2.4.C and the course skill Detect Attacks.

Materials

Room diagram (describe or sketch on paper)

A rectangular server room, 8m x 6m. One door on the south wall. One window on the east wall. An HVAC supply vent is mounted on the north wall ceiling. Two equipment racks run down the center of the room. The room is unoccupied outside business hours; no pets are permitted.

Event log (print or project)

# Sensor type Zone Cause tag True detection? Reasoning
1 PIR near window sunlight ? ?
2 PIR near door person ? ?
3 microwave near HVAC vent hvac_airflow ? ?
4 dual-tech central aisle PIR=person, MW=person ? ?
5 dual-tech central aisle PIR=person, MW=vibration ? ?
6 PIR near vent hvac_airflow ? ?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each group reviews the room diagram and marks, on paper, where they would place a motion sensor to cover the door and the central aisle without pointing directly at the window or the HVAC vent. Note any zone where a blind spot seems unavoidable.
  2. (8 min) Group works through the event log rows 1-6, deciding TRUE DETECTION or FALSE POSITIVE for each using the AND-logic rule for dual-tech sensors and the false-positive cause list from the lesson (sunlight, hvac_airflow, pet, insect, vibration, foliage).
  3. (5 min) Group decides: given the false-positive causes seen in rows 1 and 3, would you recommend upgrading the window-adjacent and vent-adjacent zones to dual-technology sensors? Justify with the AND-logic reasoning from the lesson.
  4. (4 min) Each group opens the simulation and checks their event-log verdicts against the sim's scoring for the analogous scenarios; groups revise any incorrect calls.
  5. (3 min) Report-out: each group states one sensor-placement decision and one sensitivity-tuning decision they made, and why.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one member is the "sensor installer" (proposes physical placement and mounting height), one is the "tuning engineer" (proposes sensitivity level per zone and defends it against the room's known false-positive risks), and remaining members are "auditors" (challenge whether a proposed placement or sensitivity leaves a blind spot or an unacceptable false-positive rate). For row 5 (dual-tech with a mixed cause), the group must specifically debate whether the AND-logic call (false positive) is the right operational response, or whether a person should still check the room in person.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes one sentence: "Event X is a [true detection / false positive] because [AND-logic reasoning or false-positive cause tag], and I would [keep / change] the sensor's placement or sensitivity because [reasoning]."
