2.2 Activities

Physical Security Breaches · 3

Skill 2.2.a · pairs · 25 min · Offline: yes

Unauthorized physical access (tailgating, piggybacking, lock bypass)

Goal

Students analyze a printed building access log, identify records that indicate tailgating or impossible-speed violations, and propose one countermeasure for each flagged event. Ties to LO 2.2.A (identify indicators of unauthorized physical access) and 2.2.B (explain how physical attacks bypass access controls).

Materials

Printed log (use if machines unavailable)

Event ID Time Badge ID Door Result Notes
E001 08:03:11 B-204 D1 GRANTED
E002 08:03:14 B-204 D2 GRANTED D2 is 50 m away
E003 08:07:45 B-117 D1 GRANTED
E004 08:07:48 NONE D1 — sensor: 2 people
E005 08:22:01 B-099 D3 DENIED
E006 08:22:04 B-099 D3 DENIED
E007 08:22:07 B-099 D3 GRANTED badge not changed

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (10 min) Each pair reviews the log table (or uses the simulation). For each event, decide: legitimate, suspicious, or impossible. Write "L", "S", or "I" next to the event ID and a one-sentence reason.
  2. (8 min) For every suspicious or impossible event, write the name of the attack technique it most likely represents (tailgating, piggybacking, RFID cloning, lock bypass, etc.) and one specific countermeasure the organization could deploy.
  3. (7 min) Pairs compare findings with the pair next to them. Resolve any disagreements: if you classified the same event differently, which argument is stronger?

Collaborate

Assign roles: one partner is the "security analyst" (flags records and names the attack), the other is the "CISO" (approves or challenges each flag and selects the countermeasure). Swap roles halfway through the log.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: the event ID they found most ambiguous, the attack technique it represents, and the countermeasure they would implement, in two sentences or fewer.

Skill 2.2.b · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Environmental vulnerabilities (power, HVAC, fire, water)

Goal

Groups act as a facilities/security triage team reviewing a day's worth of sensor alerts for a fictional data center, decide which alerts require immediate escalation, and propose a control for each category of environmental risk. Ties to LO 2.2.A (analyze environmental vulnerabilities) and the course skill Analyze Risk.

Materials

Sensor log (print or project)

# Room Sensor type Reading Escalate? Likely cause Control
1 server-room-a power 0% (outage) ? ? ?
2 server-room-a temperature 29°C ? ? ?
3 server-room-a humidity 66% ? ? ?
4 server-room-b smoke detected ? ? ?
5 server-room-b water detected ? ? ?
6 server-room-c temperature 22°C ? ? ?
7 server-room-c humidity 48% ? ? ?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each group reads readings 1-3 (server-room-a) and decides individually which readings should escalate to on-call staff immediately.
  2. (5 min) Group discusses: readings 2 and 3 are both elevated in the same room, what does that combination suggest that either reading alone would not? Reach consensus and fill in "likely cause" and "control" for rows 1-3.
  3. (5 min) Repeat for readings 4-5 (server-room-b) and 6-7 (server-room-c), including deciding whether row 6-7 needs any escalation at all.
  4. (5 min) Each group opens the simulation and checks their triage decisions against the sim's scoring for the analogous scenarios; groups revise any incorrect calls.
  5. (5 min) Report-out: each group states one row where they initially disagreed internally and how they resolved it.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one member is the "on-call engineer" (decides whether to escalate and to whom), one is the "budget owner" (must justify the cost of any proposed control against the risk it reduces), and remaining members are "auditors" (challenge whether the proposed control actually addresses the stated cause). For row 1 (total power outage), the group must specifically debate UPS runtime versus generator start time and what the acceptable outage window is before escalation.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes one sentence: "Reading X should [escalate / not escalate] because [reasoning tied to threshold or combined-signal rule], and [control] would reduce recurrence by [mechanism]."

Skill 2.2.c · pairs · 20 min · Offline: yes

Device theft and tampering

Goal

Pairs act as the IT security triage desk, reviewing a stack of device incident reports (thefts and tampering findings) and assigning each a risk level with a justification tied to encryption status, seal status, and detection delay. Ties to LO 2.2.B, 2.2.C and the course skill Analyze Risk.

Materials

Incident sheet (print or project)

# Type Encrypted? Seal intact? Sensitive data? Hours since check Risk level Justification
1 theft No Yes Yes 2 ? ?
2 theft Yes Yes Yes 70 ? ?
3 theft Yes Yes No 100 ? ?
4 tampering Yes No No 3 ? ?
5 tampering Yes Yes No 60 ? ?
6 tampering Yes Yes No 12 ? ?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each pair reads incidents 1-3 (theft reports) independently and writes their risk level and one-sentence justification for each.
  2. (5 min) Partners compare answers. Where they disagree, each must defend their call by citing the specific rule (encryption status, sensitivity, hours since check). Reach consensus.
  3. (5 min) Repeat for incidents 4-6 (tampering reports), paying attention to why incident 5 is riskier than incident 6 despite both having an intact seal.
  4. (5 min) Each pair opens the simulation and checks their triage decisions against the sim's scoring for equivalent scenarios; revise any incorrect calls and note which rule they misapplied.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one partner is the "incident responder" (assigns the risk level and decides who to notify), the other is the "policy auditor" (checks whether the responder's justification actually matches the written rule rather than a gut feeling). For incident 5, the pair must specifically discuss why an intact seal is not sufficient evidence that no tampering occurred. Swap roles halfway through.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes one sentence: "Incident X is [risk level] because [rule applied], and [control] would have reduced the risk by [mechanism]."
