Teacher case: authentication · written tutorial

Password spraying at the library portal

Beginner · about 20 min

Nobody was locked out, yet a library member's account was taken over. Find the slow password spray, the one account it opened and what the attacker changed.

WalkthroughPracticeSolo

Friday 6 Nov, 09:02. A member of Riverside Public Library says her portal account was changed overnight. The portal locks an account after 5 wrong passwords, and nobody was locked out. So how did someone get in?

The evidence is in ~/case (Linux) and case/ (Python). The network tools (nmap, curl, dig, whois, openssl and more) work on a recorded copy of the case network; nothing leaves your browser.

What you'll learn

  • The difference between brute force (many passwords, one account) and spraying (one password, many accounts)
  • Why per-account lockout misses spraying
  • Count with cut, sort and uniq to see the pattern

How to use this runbook

  • Run a cell with Shift+Enter (or its Run button). Run the cells in order: the first Linux cell copies the evidence into ~/case.
  • Tick each - [ ] box when you have done it.
  • Stuck? Open a Hint under the step.
  • Type your answer into each check box and it tells you if you are right. Answers are short: an address, a name, a number or a time such as 09:05.
  • Write your notes under each step. Download .ipynb keeps your notes and every output.

5. Contain, fix, learn

  • ☐ Lock k.osei, restore her email address, reset her password, tell her what was seen (412 loans exported)
  • ☐ Block 203.0.113.50 and add a per-address limit (many accounts failing from one address)
  • ☐ Check new passwords against breached and common lists; offer MFA to everyone
  • ☐ Alert when one address fails on more than 10 different accounts in an hour

Hint: Lockout per account stops brute force. Spraying needs a limit per address and a check on common passwords.
