Recover real hashes with a wordlist, then watch salting and stretching defeat the same attack.

You are the defender studying how an attacker turns a stolen hash back into a password. The cracking below is real: your browser hashes each word in a wordlist and compares it to the leaked digests, exactly as hashcat or John the Ripper would. You will crack a batch of fast unsalted hashes, then see why the same wordlist barely dents salted and key-stretched storage. The defensive lesson is the timing difference.

What the timing proved

Unsalted SHA-256 is a fast hash: a modern machine computes billions per second, so a wordlist of common passwords is exhausted instantly and identical passwords share a digest. A unique salt per user removes the shared-digest shortcut, so precomputed rainbow tables are useless and every account must be attacked on its own. Key stretching (PBKDF2, bcrypt, scrypt, Argon2) then makes each single guess thousands of times more expensive on purpose, so even a short wordlist becomes slow to run. Strong, unique passwords plus a salted, stretched hash is the defense named in LO 1.2.B and detailed in topic 4.2.
