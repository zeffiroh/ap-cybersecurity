Why accuracy lies on rare events

When attacks are rare, a detector can score 99% accuracy just by being right about the flood of benign traffic, while its alert queue is almost entirely false positives, a direct consequence of the base rate. Precision (how many alerts are real) and recall (how many attacks are caught) are the honest measures, and tightening a rule trades one for the other. Automating the response, blocking on every alert, multiplies the cost of each false positive into a real outage, which is why LO 1.5.A treats a generated detection rule as useful only when its precision and its response action are matched to the base rate, not when its accuracy looks high.
