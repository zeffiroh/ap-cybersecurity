1.3 Activities

Public Network Sniffing · 4

Skill 1.3.a · pairs · 20 min · Offline: yes

Adversary skill levels (low-skilled vs high-skilled / zero-days); motivation

Goal

Students analyze adversary profiles, assign skill tiers, and justify their reasoning using the feature-based classification rules from the lesson. Ties to LO 1.3.A (assess adversary capability and motivation to analyze risk).

Materials

Profile cards (print or display)

Profile Uses zero-day Custom tooling Targets specific victim Off-the-shelf only Adapts to defenses
Alex No No No Yes No
Blake Yes Yes Yes No Yes
Casey No No Yes No Yes
Dana No Yes No No No
Emery No No Yes Yes No

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each pair opens the simulation and sorts the on-screen adversary profiles into LOW and HIGH tiers, recording their reasoning for each.
  2. After completing the sim, pairs apply the same rules to the five profile cards above and record a tier and one-sentence justification for each.
  3. Pairs compare answers with the pair next to them. For any disagreement, they cite the specific rule from interface.md that resolves the dispute.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one partner is the "threat analyst" (applies the rules mechanically), the other is the "risk manager" (challenges edge cases and asks "what if one feature changed?"). Swap roles for the second half of the profile cards.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: the tier they assigned to Casey (profile 3) and the rule number from interface.md that determined it, plus one sentence explaining why motivation (the goal of the attack) matters when assigning risk level.

Skill 1.3.b · pairs · 25 min · Offline: yes

Evil twin attack

Goal

Students practice the core analytic skill: given a raw wireless scan table (SSID, BSSID, security level, signal strength), identify which entries are suspicious and justify each flagged entry by citing a specific indicator. Ties to LO 1.3.B (identify indicators of evil-twin attacks on public Wi-Fi).

Materials

Printed scan table

# SSID BSSID Security Signal
1 CafeGuest AA:BB:CC:11:22:33 WPA2 -55
2 CafeGuest DE:AD:BE:EF:00:01 OPEN -42
3 Airport-Free 00:11:22:33:44:55 OPEN -70
4 Airport-Free FF:EE:DD:CC:BB:AA OPEN -48
5 CorpNet 0A:1B:2C:3D:4E:5F WPA3 -60
6 HotelLobby 11:22:33:44:55:66 WPA2 -65

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each pair reads the scan table silently and writes down which rows they think are suspicious, without discussing yet.
  2. (8 min) Partners compare their lists. For each disagreement, argue your case using the detection rules from atom.md: same SSID / different BSSID, or same SSID / weaker security. Agree on a final answer.
  3. (7 min) Open the simulation. Work through its scan rounds. Check whether your reasoning from the table translates, the sim uses the same analytic rules.
  4. (5 min) One partner reads from the scan table, acting as the "attacker", they explain why their evil-twin entry (row 2 or 4) would fool a casual user. The other partner explains the defensive response.

Collaborate

Assign roles: one partner is the WIDS analyst (identifies and justifies flags), the other is the attacker (argues why each evil twin is convincing to a victim). Swap roles for the second round. Both record: indicator observed, reason it is suspicious, defensive action.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits one written sentence per suspicious row: name the row, the specific indicator (duplicate BSSID, security downgrade, etc.), and one action a user could take to avoid connecting to that AP.

Skill 1.3.c · pairs · 20 min · Offline: yes

Jamming / denial-of-service attack

Goal

Students practice reading a noise-floor sample series and applying the consecutive-threshold detection rule to classify each series as "jamming" or "clean." They also practice reasoning about why the rule is tuned (what happens when T or N is too low or too high). Ties to LO 1.3.B (identify indicators of wireless denial-of-service attacks).

Materials

Printed sample series

Detection rule (from interface.md): T = 80, N = 5 (a sample series flags as jamming if 5 or more consecutive samples exceed 80)

Series Samples Jamming?
A 20, 25, 90, 22, 30, 18, 24 ?
B 15, 82, 85, 88, 91, 83, 87, 20 ?
C 70, 72, 75, 71, 73, 74, 76 ?
D 50, 81, 82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 87, 88, 30 ?
E 90, 91, 92, 88, 87, 86, 85, 84, 83, 82, 25, 10 ?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each student reads the table individually and classifies each series using the rule T = 80, N = 5. No discussion yet, write down "jamming" or "clean" with a one-sentence justification citing the consecutive-sample run they found.
  2. (7 min) Compare with your partner. For any disagreement, step through the series together sample by sample, counting the run. Agree on a final answer for each.
  3. (5 min) Open the simulation. Toggle the simulated jammer and observe how the noise floor changes. Discuss: could you distinguish a jammer from a microwave oven in the sim data?
  4. (3 min) Optional extension: what would happen to false-positive and false-negative rates if you changed N from 5 to 2? If you raised T from 80 to 95?

Collaborate

Assign roles: one partner is the network monitor (reads and classifies the data), the other is the analyst (explains why the rule fires or does not fire for each series). After the table, swap: analyst proposes a new sample series, monitor classifies it.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student writes the detection rule from memory (threshold value, minimum consecutive samples, and the Boolean return value) and gives one example of a real-world event that could cause a true positive versus a false positive using this rule.

Skill 1.3.d · small groups (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

War driving; protecting yourself (verify SSIDs, avoid open networks, VPN)

Goal

Students apply the three-step personal safety protocol (verify SSID, avoid open networks, use VPN) to a set of network scenarios and produce a risk-flag report for each. They also discuss how to respond when no safe option exists. Ties to LO 1.3.B (identify indicators of wireless attack exposure) and 1.3.C (describe mitigation strategies for public-Wi-Fi risk).

Materials

Scenario cards

Card 1, Airport departure lounge Network visible: "AirportFreeWifi" (OPEN). You did not ask staff. You have your VPN installed but it is not connected. You need to check work email. Question: What flags does this network carry? What do you do?

Card 2, Hotel lobby Network: "HotelGuest" (WPA2). You confirmed the name with the front desk. Your VPN is active. Question: What flags (if any)? Is it safe to proceed?

Card 3, Coffee shop Network: "CafeNet" (WPA2). You did not ask staff. Your VPN is active. Question: What flags? What is the residual risk?

Card 4, Study hall Network: "StudyNet" (OPEN). You confirmed the SSID with the librarian. You have no VPN. Question: What flags? What is the safest course of action?

Card 5, Street outside a building Your device auto-connected to "CorporateWifi" (WPA2) while you walked past. You did not verify the SSID. Your VPN is not running. Question: Should you continue using this connection? What flags apply?

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. (5 min) Each group reads all five cards and assigns a preliminary flag list to each.
  2. (10 min) Groups use the simulation to run each scenario through the sim's risk assessor. Compare sim output to the group's preliminary assessment. Reconcile any differences.
  3. (5 min) Each group selects the scenario where the user faces the hardest trade-off (no good option) and drafts a written recommendation: what to do, what not to do, and why.
  4. (5 min) One group presents their recommendation; others add or challenge.

Collaborate

Assign roles per group: risk analyst (applies the flag rules), user advocate (argues for usability, what if you really must connect?), security officer (enforces policy regardless of inconvenience). Rotate roles across cards.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits a three-sentence personal Wi-Fi policy they would follow on a school trip: one sentence per step (verify, avoid open, VPN).
