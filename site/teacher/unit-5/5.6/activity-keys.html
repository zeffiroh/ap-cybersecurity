Topic 5.6 Activity Answer Keys

Application Threat Auditing · instructor-only · 3 entries

Skill 5.6.a

Application monitoring and logging

Instructor notes

  • Expected reasoning: students should identify the qualifying 60-second window and count exactly 5+ 401 events inside it, not just "a lot of failures somewhere in the log."
  • Common stumble: students count all events in the whole log rather than checking whether any single 60-second window meets the threshold; walk through the sliding-window idea explicitly.
  • Feeds the coding problem's brute-force detector and the unit's SOC-analyst framing.
Skill 5.6.b

Data loss detection (DLP)

Instructor notes

  • Expected reasoning: internal/known-partner systems belong on the allowlist; unfamiliar external hosts do not, regardless of transfer size.
  • Common stumble: students treat "big transfer = always bad" without checking the destination first, or vice versa, both signals (size AND destination) matter together, not separately.
  • Feeds the coding problem's DLP egress rule and previews "low and slow" exfiltration, a common advanced-attacker technique, for later discussion.
Skill 5.6.c

Digital evidence analysis and attack classification

Instructor notes

  • Expected reasoning: students should cite specific evidence items, not just restate the category name, and should recognize when a bundle is ambiguous (e.g. mixed weak signals) rather than forcing false confidence.
  • Common stumble: students anchor on the first indicator they read rather than weighing the full bundle; prompt them to list every piece of evidence before concluding.
  • Feeds the coding problem's attack classifier and the unit's incident-response tabletop.
