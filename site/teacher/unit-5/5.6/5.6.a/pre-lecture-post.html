Application monitoring and logging

Unit 5 · Securing Applications and Data · 5.6 Application Threat Auditing

Before class today. Applications are not silent. Every time a user logs in, a form is submitted, a file is opened, or an error is thrown, the application can record a structured line in its log, a timestamped, machine-readable trace of what just happened. Application monitoring is the practice of collecting and watching those traces in real time; logging is the act of writing them down so they can be examined later.

The intuition to hold onto: The line worth holding onto walking in: you cannot detect an attack you never recorded, which means logging isn't a forensic afterthought, it's the precondition for every detection technique the rest of this skill and the next two are about to cover. A single failed login is meaningless noise, people mistype passwords constantly. Fifty failed logins across twenty usernames from one IP in two minutes is an unmistakable pattern. The shift from "reading individual events" to "reading the shape of many events together" is the entire skill this skill is building.

A myth worth busting before we start: "If no alarm fired, nothing bad happened." → Alarms only fire on configured rules; a misconfigured or absent rule creates a silent blind spot. Periodic manual log review is still necessary.

Today's goal: Explain what application logging captures, describe how security teams use log analysis to detect attacks in progress, and identify the key log fields and patterns that signal brute-force, injection, and account-takeover activity. Handout: pre-lecture-handout.docx.
