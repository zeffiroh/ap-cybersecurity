Data loss detection (DLP)

Unit 5 · Securing Applications and Data · 5.6 Application Threat Auditing

Before class today. An organization can defend its perimeter, patch its applications, and monitor its logs, and still lose its most valuable asset: the data itself, quietly walking out the door.

The intuition to hold onto: The pairing worth carrying into this lecture: DLP fights the exact same battle as 5.6.a's log analysis, but content-based detection and volume/behavior-based detection split the work the way SAST and DAST split application testing in 5.5.c, one looks at what is moving, the other at how much and how oddly it's moving, and each catches what the other misses. An attacker who encrypts stolen data before exfiltrating it defeats content inspection completely, but a 3 AM upload of two gigabytes to a domain nobody's ever used before still lights up a behavior-based system, because that system never needed to understand the content at all.

A myth worth busting before we start: "DLP only needs to scan for keywords like 'confidential'." → Content inspection is only half the picture; volume/behavior-based detection catches exfiltration that has no recognizable content pattern, such as encrypted or compressed data.
