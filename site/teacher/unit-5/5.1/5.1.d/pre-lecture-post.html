Data breaches and their impact

Unit 5 · Securing Applications and Data · 5.1 Application Code Vulnerabilities

Before class today. A data breach is any incident in which sensitive, protected, or confidential data is accessed, disclosed, or exfiltrated by someone who is not authorized to have it.

The intuition to hold onto: The habit worth building before this lecture: stop asking "did a breach happen" and start asking "how bad is this specific breach," because the AP framework wants you to reason quantitatively, not just spot the incident. Sensitivity, scale, and time-to-detect are three independent dials, and a breach can be severe by maxing out just one of them, forty leaked health records can outrank four million leaked newsletter emails even though the second number is a thousand times bigger. That's the same risk-equals-likelihood-times-impact logic from way back in Unit 2, just now applied to data instead of physical assets.

A myth worth busting before we start: "A breach only matters if passwords were stolen." → Exposure of PII, financial, or health data alone (without passwords) can enable identity theft and fraud and often carries stronger legal notification obligations.

Today's goal: Explain what a data breach is, identify the categories of data whose exposure causes the most harm, and estimate the severity of a breach from the type of data exposed, the number of records affected, and how quickly it was detected/contained. Handout: pre-lecture-handout.docx.
