Roles, permissions, and least privilege

Unit 5 · Securing Applications and Data · 5.2 Linux Permission Controls

Nice work today. Here's what to lock in:

  • Recap handout: post-lecture-handout.docx
  • Simulation: SimLab
  • Self-check quiz: due before next class

The big picture: The phrase worth keeping is blast radius, because it reframes why least privilege matters in a way that connects straight back to 5.2.a and forward to 5.2.c. A role isn't just a convenience for onboarding new hires, it's a pre-committed answer to "how much damage does one phished account cause," decided in advance, before any attack happens. A tightly scoped support-agent role turns a phishing success into a contained incident; a role padded with "just in case" permissions turns the exact same phishing email into a much larger breach. That's not a hypothetical, it's literally the mechanism 5.2.c is about to walk through in detail: lateral movement is what happens when blast radius wasn't actually contained, and every silent excess permission this skill warns about is a potential hop in that chain.

Key terms to lock in:

  • Permission: a single narrow grant to perform one action on one resource type.
  • Role: a named bundle of permissions assigned to users as a unit (RBAC).
  • Least privilege: granting a subject only the permissions it needs, nothing more.
  • Privilege creep: the gradual, unnoticed accumulation of unnecessary permissions over time.
  • Role review / recertification: periodic re-confirmation that role assignments are still justified, used to counteract privilege creep.
  • Separation of duties: splitting a sensitive process across multiple roles so no single role can complete it alone.

Common misconceptions to watch for:

  • "Giving extra access 'just in case' is harmless if it's never used." → Unused excess permission is exactly what increases blast radius if that account is ever compromised, it is a silent risk, not a neutral one.
  • "Once a role is set up correctly, it stays correct." → Job functions and org structure change continuously; without periodic review, roles drift away from least privilege via creep.

Learning objective(s): 5.2.A, 5.2.B
