1.5.d, Faster, more accurate threat detection and response

Before class today. A security operations center is always racing against the clock. Every minute an attacker spends inside a network is another minute of data exfiltration, lateral movement, or credential harvesting. The time from the moment an incident is detected to the moment it is resolved, Mean Time to Respond, or MTTR, is one of the most-watched metrics in cyber defense.

The intuition to hold onto: MTTR is the metric that makes an abstract idea, "attackers race against defenders", measurable and manageable. The intuition worth internalizing: every second an incident sits unactioned isn't neutral time, it's time an attacker is actively spending on lateral movement or exfiltration. Decomposing MTTR into detect/triage/contain/eradicate isn't just accounting, it tells you exactly where to invest, because AI helps each sub-metric through a different mechanism (continuous monitoring shrinks detection time; severity scoring shrinks triage time; automated playbooks shrink containment time).

A myth worth busting before we start: "Lower MTTR always means better security." → MTTR must be balanced against accuracy. A system that auto-resolves everything has a very low MTTR but will also close real incidents prematurely. Precision matters.

Today's goal: Explain how AI reduces Mean Time to Respond (MTTR) in a security operations center, implement an incident-triage algorithm that prioritizes incidents by severity and computes response-time metrics, and articulate the difference between automated resolution and human-escalated response.
