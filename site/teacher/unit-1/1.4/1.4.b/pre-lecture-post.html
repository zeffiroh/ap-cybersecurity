Generative-AI phishing; prompt injection against LLM apps

Unit 1 · Introduction to Security · 1.4 AI-Augmented Cyberattacks

Before class today. Traditional phishing campaigns have always had a tell: volume. Sending ten thousand emails required a template, and templates left seams, awkward phrasing, inconsistent grammar, generic greetings, mismatched signatures. Spam filters learned to score those signals.

The intuition to hold onto: The core economic insight here is worth sitting with because it explains an entire generation of security tooling becoming obsolete overnight: spam filters were never detecting maliciousness directly, they were detecting the fingerprints of mass production, awkward phrasing, generic greetings, inconsistent grammar. An LLM doesn't just write better phishing emails, it removes the fingerprint the filter was actually keying on. When the cost of a personalized, fluent, contextually accurate message drops to zero, "this reads a little off" stops being a signal you can rely on at all.

A myth worth busting before we start: "AI-generated phishing is easy to spot because it sounds robotic." → Current LLMs produce text that is more fluent than many humans' writing. Stylistic detection is not reliable.

Today's goal: Identify prompt-injection patterns in user input and explain how generative AI lowers the cost of targeted phishing by bypassing grammar-based detection. Handout: pre-lecture-handout.docx.
