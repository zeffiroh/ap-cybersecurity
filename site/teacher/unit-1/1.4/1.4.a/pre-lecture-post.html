1.4.a, Voice/image cloning for impersonation

Before class today. For decades, the phrase "I heard his voice, so I knew it was him" carried real weight. A voice on the phone, a face on a video call, or a signature on an image felt like trustworthy proof of identity. That assumption is now dangerously fragile.

The intuition to hold onto: The unsettling reframe this skill asks you to make: for your entire life, hearing a familiar voice or seeing a familiar face has been evidence of identity, strong enough evidence that we built our entire social trust system on it. That evidence just became forgeable at consumer-hardware cost. The professor's intuition here is to stop asking "does this sound/look real to me?" (a losing arms race against improving models) and start asking "does this interaction include something a clone couldn't have access to?", a shared secret the model was never trained on, a callback to a number you already had.

A myth worth busting before we start: "I can always tell if a voice is fake." → Current cloning quality fools trained humans the majority of the time. Perceptual detection is not a reliable control.
