False information in LLM training sets (data poisoning)

Unit 1 · Introduction to Security · 1.4 AI-Augmented Cyberattacks

Before class today. Machine learning models learn entirely from their training data. If that data is wrong, the model will be wrong in exactly the way the attacker intended. Data poisoning is the deliberate act of inserting mislabeled, corrupted, or specially crafted samples into a training dataset so that the resulting model behaves in a way the attacker chooses.

The intuition to hold onto: Data poisoning asks you to think about security in a place most people never consider a threat surface at all: the raw material a model learns from, before the model even exists. The intuition that makes this click is realizing a model has no concept of "training" versus "deployment" separate from the numbers it was shown, if the numbers say hostile language correlates with the label "positive," the model doesn't know that's wrong, it just knows that's what the data said. Garbage in isn't just garbage out; carefully crafted garbage in is a chosen out.

A myth worth busting before we start: "Poisoning requires access to the model." → Poisoning targets the training data, which may be collected from public sources, crowd-sourced platforms, or user submissions, no model access needed.

Today's goal: Identify indicators of data poisoning in a labeled training dataset and explain how poisoned data degrades or manipulates AI model behavior. Handout: pre-lecture-handout.docx.
