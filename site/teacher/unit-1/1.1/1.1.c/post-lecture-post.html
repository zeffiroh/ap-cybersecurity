## 1.1.c recap, Email, text, and social media attacks
Nice work today. Here's what to lock in:

- Recap handout: `post-lecture-handout.docx`
- Simulation: SimLab
- Self-check quiz: due before next class

**The big picture:** The multi-channel "pre-warning" pattern described here, a text that says "expect an email from us" followed by the actual phishing email, is worth sitting with, because it's a small example of a much bigger idea you'll see again in this course: attackers build *social proof* across independent-seeming signals to defeat verification. The single defense that generalizes across every channel is the same one from 1.1.a: verify through a path the message itself didn't give you. Look up the number yourself. Don't use the one in the text.

**Key terms to lock in:**
- **Phishing**, a mass social-engineering attack via email (or another channel) that impersonates a trusted entity to harvest credentials or deliver malware.
- **Spear phishing**, a targeted phishing attack crafted using intelligence about a specific person or organization.
- **Whaling**, spear phishing directed at high-value targets such as executives.
- **Smishing**, phishing delivered via SMS (text message).
- **Social media impersonation**, creating a fake account that closely mimics a real person or organization to deceive their contacts.
- **Business email compromise (BEC)**, an attack in which the attacker impersonates an executive or business partner to authorize fraudulent transfers or disclose sensitive data.

**Common misconceptions -- don't fall for these:**
- "I can always tell a phishing email by its grammar." → Spear phishing and AI-generated phishing (1.4.b) can be grammatically perfect and contextually accurate. Grammar is an unreliable signal.
- "Only email matters; texts are safe." → Smishing is growing rapidly and often bypasses the security awareness training that focuses on email.
- "Checking the sender name is enough." → Display names are trivially spoofed. Always inspect the actual sending address, not just what appears in the "From:" field.
