Topic 1.1 Activity Answer Keys

Social Engineering · instructor-only · 3 entries

Skill 1.1.a

Psychological tactics (intimidation, urgency, authority, scarcity)

Instructor notes

  • Expected tactics map to the four labels: urgency, authority, scarcity, intimidation.
  • Common stumble: students conflate scarcity ("only 3 left") with urgency ("act in 1 hour"). Clarify scarcity = limited supply, urgency = limited time.
  • Feeds the unit project (phishing analyzer) and scenario stage 1 (social-engineering entry).
Skill 1.1.b

Elicitation; malicious links and files

Instructor notes

  • Sim target score is 5 out of 7 messages; most pairs reach it in 10-12 minutes.
  • Common stumble on URLs: students read left-to-right and see the trusted brand first, missing that the actual registrable domain (rightmost before the first slash) is attacker-controlled. Reinforce the right-to-left reading rule explicitly.
  • Common stumble on elicitation: students think they "gave nothing away" but the conversation may have revealed org chart details, software names, or processes. Debrief by listing every specific fact Partner B disclosed during the role-play.
  • Feeds the unit project (phishing analyzer) and scenario stage 1 (attacker uses elicitation to map Meridian Health's infrastructure before sending a malicious link).
Skill 1.1.c

Email, text, and social media attacks

Instructor notes

  • Expected indicator assignments:
  • Card A: lookalike-domain (amaz0n-security.net), credential-request, mismatched-sender, shortened-link (bit.ly)
  • Card B: shortened-link (bare IP 192.168.0.22), credential-request (reroute = verify)
  • Card C: lookalike-domain (trailing underscore handle), credential-request, shortened-link (cutt.ly), mismatched-sender (Netflix not in sender handle)
  • Card D: urgent-money-request, (no shortened link, no lookalike, BEC relies on authority + secrecy, not technical deception)
  • Card E: none, legitimate family text
  • Common stumbles: students label Card D with lookalike-domain because the domain looks odd; push them to apply the defined rule (is a known brand substituted with look-alike chars?). Card E stumps some students who expect every card to have an indicator.
  • The BEC pattern on Card D (bypass normal process + urgency + secrecy) is the hardest to detect technically, discuss why procedural controls (dual-authorization for wires) matter.
  • Feeds the unit project (phishing analyzer) and scenario stage 1 (social-engineering entry).
