Nice work today. Here's what to lock in:

  • Recap handout: post-lecture-handout.docx
  • Simulation: SimLab
  • Self-check quiz: due before next class

The big picture: Notice that not one of these four tactics required the attacker to know anything about computers. That's the whole point of starting the course here: before you learn to harden a server or read a packet capture, you need to internalize that the cheapest, most reliable attack surface in any organization is a human being's nervous system under pressure. Every technical control you'll learn for the rest of this course, MFA, segmentation, detection rules, exists downstream of an attacker successfully doing what we practiced today. Getting this instinct right early pays off in every unit that follows.

Key terms to lock in:

  • Social engineering: manipulating people (not machines) into actions that aid an attacker.
  • Urgency: manufactured time pressure that suppresses careful verification.
  • Authority: borrowed legitimacy (boss, IT, government) used to compel compliance.
  • Scarcity: implied limited availability that triggers fear of missing out.
  • Intimidation: threats that create fear so the target complies to remove the threat.

Common misconceptions to watch for:

  • "Only careless people fall for it." → Anyone under stress, time pressure, or distraction is vulnerable; tactics target universal psychology, not intelligence.
  • "If it has a logo, it's real." → Logos and sender names are trivially spoofed; verify the request, not the branding.

Learning objective(s): 1.1.A, 1.1.B
