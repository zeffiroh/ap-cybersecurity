1.3.d, War driving; protecting yourself (verify SSIDs, avoid open networks, VPN)

Before class today. War driving is the practice of driving or walking through a geographic area with a laptop, smartphone, or dedicated wireless scanner to discover, log, and map Wi-Fi networks.

The intuition to hold onto: War driving is worth understanding less as an attack in itself and more as reconnaissance infrastructure, the map an attacker builds before deciding where to strike. What makes it unsettling is how little it costs: the tool that once required a car, a directional antenna, and real dedication is now a free app on a phone already in everyone's pocket. The barrier to finding a target has collapsed even where the barrier to exploiting one hasn't.

A myth worth busting before we start: "A password-protected network means I am safe." → WPA2 encrypts the air interface, but the access point operator can still read unencrypted traffic, and a compromised or malicious AP can intercept HTTPS via SSL stripping. A VPN provides an additional layer.

Today's goal: Explain what war driving is and why it enables targeted wireless attacks, evaluate a Wi-Fi network against a set of risk criteria, and apply a systematic risk-flagging process to decide whether connecting is safe. Handout: pre-lecture-handout.docx.
