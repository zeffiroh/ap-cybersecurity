Network Threat Detection · instructor-only · 3 entries

Skill 3.5.a

Intrusion detection and prevention systems (IDS/IPS)

Instructor notes

  • Expected TP/FP/FN/TN answers match the sim scenario keys. Common stumble: students confuse false negative with "the negative (benign) outcome was wrong", clarify that positive/negative refers to the sensor's verdict, not the traffic type.
  • The tuning discussion usually surfaces the alert-fatigue concept naturally; guide students toward naming it explicitly.
  • Feeds the unit project (building a traffic analyzer) and scenario stage 3 (network intrusion detection).
Skill 3.5.b

Network monitoring and traffic analysis

Instructor notes

  • Key learning moment: 10.0.4.2 passes both the volume check and the port check yet is still the most dangerous entry, this demonstrates that timing-pattern (beaconing) anomalies require a different kind of analysis than simple thresholds.
  • Common stumble: students only check volume and port and miss that beaconing is about timing regularity, not amount.
  • Feeds the unit project (building a traffic analyzer) and scenario stage 3 (network intrusion detection).
Skill 3.5.c

Incident response sequence

Instructor notes

  • Card 7 is written to sound proactive ("wipe and reinstall right away") but is a containment violation if the host is still connected, reinfection or attacker awareness can result. It only belongs in Eradication AFTER Card 5 (Containment) is complete.
  • Card 9 tests whether students remember Recovery includes active monitoring, not just restoration, a system brought back online without monitoring may still be compromised.
  • Common stumble: groups place Card 2 (removing the backdoor) before Card 5 (isolating the subnet); walk them back to the general principle, contain first, always.
  • Feeds the unit project and the scenario's incident-response stage.
