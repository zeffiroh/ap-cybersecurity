3.1.b recap, DNS attacks (spoofing, cache poisoning)

  • Self-check quiz: due before next class

The big picture: The throughline worth remembering is that DNS poisoning and ARP spoofing (3.1.c, next atom) are the same underlying vulnerability, a device blindly trusting an unauthenticated reply, expressed at two different layers of the stack you just met in 3.1.a. DNS poisons the mapping from name to IP before a connection even starts; ARP spoofing poisons the mapping from IP to MAC once you're already on the local network. Keep that parallel sharp, because it's exactly the kind of connection the AP exam rewards: recognizing that "forge the trusted-but-unauthenticated reply" is a reusable attack pattern, not a one-off DNS quirk. And don't let the misconception slide by, HTTPS staying green in your browser tells you nothing about whether DNS lied to you; it only tells you the certificate matched whatever server you actually reached, which is precisely why a spoofed DNS answer pointing you to an attacker's server usually fails loudly (a cert warning) rather than silently, and why that failure is a feature of the system working, not proof DNS spoofing didn't happen.
