Alarm systems

Unit 2 · Securing Spaces · 2.4 Physical Detection Systems

Before class today. A lock keeps someone out. A camera records that they got in anyway. An alarm system sits in between: it is a detective control whose job is to notice an intrusion attempt in progress and trigger a response fast enough to matter.

The intuition to hold onto: The single most important sentence in this skill is the one about what an alarm is not: nothing about a siren physically stops anyone from walking through a broken window. An alarm is a detective control that happens to be loud, not a barrier, and confusing the two is the single most common mistake in physical security planning, because "we have an alarm" feels like it should mean "this door can't be opened" when it actually just means "someone will eventually find out it was."

A myth worth busting before we start: "An armed alarm prevents a break-in." → An alarm is a detective and response control, not a preventive one; it can only notice and react to an intrusion that has already started, not physically stop it. Preventive controls are locks, barriers, and access control.

Today's goal: Identify the components of an intrusion detection alarm system, explain how arm states and entry/exit delay affect whether a sensor trip is a real event, and classify alarm events (true alarm, false alarm, suppressed) to decide which should escalate to central-station dispatch.
