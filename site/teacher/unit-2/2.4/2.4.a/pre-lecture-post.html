Unit 2 · Securing Spaces · 2.4 Physical Detection Systems

Before class today. Physical security controls, badge readers, key-card doors, biometric scanners, generate a continuous stream of timestamped records every time someone attempts or succeeds at entering a controlled area. That stream is called a physical access log, and it is one of the most powerful detective tools a security team has.

The intuition to hold onto: The reframe worth making here: an access log isn't a record of what happened, it's a record of what a badge did, and the gap between those two things is where every interesting anomaly lives. A valid badge swipe only proves a credential was presented; it says nothing about who actually presented it. Once you internalize that distinction, the detection rules in this skill stop looking like arbitrary thresholds and start looking obvious: you're not looking for invalid credentials, you're looking for valid credentials behaving in ways a single human physically couldn't.

A myth worth busting before we start: "A valid badge swipe means the entry was authorized." → Badges can be cloned, stolen, or shared; a valid swipe only proves a credential was presented, not that the right person presented it. Context, time, location, frequency, is what determines legitimacy.
