CIA triad: confidentiality, integrity, availability

Unit 2 · Securing Spaces · 2.1 Security Foundations: CIA Triad

Before class today. Every security decision in cybersecurity traces back to three questions: Is this information being kept from those who should not see it? Has this information been changed without authorization? Can the people and systems that need this information actually reach it?

The intuition to hold onto: Here's the mental model worth carrying through the rest of the course: whenever you read about a security incident, don't ask "how bad was it," ask "which pillar did it break." Confidentiality, integrity, and availability aren't three items on a checklist, they're three completely different failure modes that call for three completely different fixes. A confidentiality fix (encrypt it) does nothing for an integrity problem (verify it hasn't changed), and neither does anything for an availability problem (make sure it's still reachable). Once you sort an incident into the right bucket, the right defense almost picks itself.

A myth worth busting before we start: "Security is mainly about keeping things secret." → Confidentiality is only one pillar; many attacks target integrity or availability and cause equal or greater harm without stealing any data.
