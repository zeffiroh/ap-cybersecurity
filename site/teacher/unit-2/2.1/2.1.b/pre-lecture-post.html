2.1.b, Threat actors and their motivations

Before class today. When Meridian University's security team arrived on Monday morning, they found three incident tickets waiting. Someone had encrypted the registrar's database and left a ransom note. An anonymous post on a public forum included thousands of student records.

The intuition to hold onto: The single habit this skill is trying to build: before you design a defense, name the attacker. "Someone might attack us" is not a threat model, "a script kiddie scanning for unpatched software" and "a nation-state after our research data" are, and they lead to opposite spending decisions. A patch cycle stops the first and barely slows the second. Get in the habit of asking who and why before you ask how, because the six categories in this skill aren't trivia to memorize, they're a sorting mechanism that tells you where your budget actually belongs.

A myth worth busting before we start: "All hackers are criminals out for money", Hacktivists and nation-state actors have entirely different goals; conflating them leads to misaligned defenses.
