Physical Security Breaches · instructor-only · 3 entries

Skill 2.2.a

Unauthorized physical access (tailgating, piggybacking, lock bypass)

Instructor notes

  • Expected flags: E001-E002 = impossible speed (B-204 cannot reach D2 in 3 s across 50 m; likely badge pass-back or clone); E003-E004 = tailgating (one swipe, sensor detects two people); E005-E007 = credential stuffing / replay, three attempts, third granted without changing the badge (may indicate a cloned badge being tried at slightly different angles, or a timing-based attack on the reader).
  • Common stumble: students may not flag E001-E002 if they do not calculate the implied walking speed. Prompt: "How fast would you need to walk to cover 50 meters in 3 seconds?"
  • Feeds the unit project (physical-pen-test report) and scenario stage 2 (attacker gaining server-room access).
Skill 2.2.b

Environmental vulnerabilities (power, HVAC, fire, water)

Answer key (instructor only)

# Escalate? Likely cause Control (sample)
1 Yes, CRITICAL Total power loss to server-room-a UPS bridges immediately; generator should start within its rated window; verify via regular load testing
2 Yes, escalates to CRITICAL once combined with #3 Rising temperature, likely HVAC strain Check chiller/HVAC unit; verify redundant cooling capacity
3 Yes, escalates to CRITICAL once combined with #2 Rising humidity alongside temperature, HVAC failure signature Same HVAC repair; humidity control tied to same system
4 Yes, CRITICAL Smoke detected, possible fire Clean-agent suppression should trigger; evacuate; do not use water
5 Yes, CRITICAL Water/moisture detected at floor level Investigate plumbing above/near room; raised flooring should have limited spread
6 No, NORMAL Temperature within normal operating range No action; continue monitoring
7 No, NORMAL Humidity within normal operating range No action; continue monitoring

Instructor notes

  • Timing: rows 1, 4, 5 are unambiguous single-sensor CRITICAL cases; rows 2-3 are the intended teaching moment for the combined-signal / HVAC-failure pattern from the lesson.
  • Common stumble: students treat 2 and 3 as independent WARNING-level readings and under-escalate; push them to explain why the combination is worse than either alone (this mirrors the escalation rule implemented in problem/).
  • Rows 6-7 exist to test whether groups over-escalate normal readings out of caution; a mature risk analysis should not treat everything as an emergency.
  • Feeds the unit project (threat modeling a small network/facility) and connects forward to 2.3.d (environmental controls) and 2.4 (detecting physical attacks).
Skill 2.2.c

Device theft and tampering

Answer key (instructor only)

# Risk level Justification
1 HIGH Device not encrypted, data is exposed with no cryptographic barrier regardless of anything else.
2 MEDIUM Encrypted, but sensitive data and 70 hours since check exceeds the 48-hour threshold, long detection delay raises risk.
3 LOW Encrypted and not flagged as sensitive; long delay does not matter because the sensitivity condition is not met.
4 HIGH Seal not intact, direct physical evidence the enclosure was opened.
5 MEDIUM Seal intact, but 60 hours since check exceeds the 48-hour threshold, tampering that doesn't require opening the case (e.g., USB implant) could have gone unnoticed that long.
6 LOW Seal intact and only 12 hours since check, within the normal monitoring window.

Instructor notes

  • Timing: incidents 1 and 4 are unambiguous HIGH cases (clear rule violation); 2/5 are the intended teaching moment for the "detection delay" MEDIUM rule; 3/6 are the LOW baseline.
  • Common stumble: students want to mark incident 3 as MEDIUM because "it was a theft," without checking that contains_sensitive_data is False, the MEDIUM rule for theft requires both conditions (sensitive AND long delay), not just a long delay.
  • Reinforce that an intact seal is reassuring but not proof of no tampering, which is why the hours-since-check rule exists independently of the seal check.
  • Feeds the unit project (threat modeling a small network/facility) and connects forward to 2.3 (protecting physical spaces) and 2.4 (detecting physical attacks).
