2.2.b

Environmental vulnerabilities (power, HVAC, fire, water)

Unit 2 · Securing Spaces · 2.2 Physical Security Breaches

Before class today. Locks and badge readers defend against a person who wants to walk into your server room. But a server room can be destroyed just as thoroughly by things that are never "unauthorized" in the access-control sense at all: a power outage, an overheated rack, a fire, or a burst pipe.

The intuition to hold onto: The intuition worth keeping: nature doesn't care about your threat model. A ransomware gang wants something from you; a burst pipe doesn't want anything, it just happens, and the server room goes offline exactly the same way either way. That's the whole reason this skill exists in a cybersecurity course at all: if your risk analysis only counts threats with intent behind them, you've quietly excluded some of the most common causes of real outages, and "but nobody attacked us" is cold comfort to a hospital that just lost patient records to a flooded basement.

A myth worth busting before we start: "Environmental risk isn't really a cybersecurity topic." → A power, cooling, fire, or water failure produces the same availability (and sometimes integrity) loss as a targeted cyberattack; AP Cybersecurity's Analyze Risk skill treats all threats to CIA equally regardless of whether an adversary is involved.

Today's goal: Identify how power loss, HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning) failure, fire, and water/flood events threaten the availability and integrity of physical IT infrastructure, and you can evaluate whether a facility's environmental sensor readings indicate a developing incident.
