2.3.d, Environmental controls (HVAC, fire suppression, power)

Before class today. Physical hardware is the foundation of every digital system. A server does not care whether its data was stolen by a hacker or destroyed because a room hit 50 degrees Celsius, the outcome is the same: the data is gone and the service is unavailable.

The intuition to hold onto: The line worth remembering from this skill: a server doesn't know or care whether it went offline because of a hacker or a broken thermostat, the outcome is identical, so your risk analysis should weigh both equally. That's the entire justification for treating HVAC, fire suppression, and power as cybersecurity topics rather than "facilities problems." If your job is protecting availability, the source of the threat is irrelevant; only the probability and the consequence matter, and a badly maintained cooling system can take down more systems in an afternoon than most attackers manage in a year.

A myth worth busting before we start: Sprinkler systems are fine for data centers -> Water damages electronics on contact and even a false activation can destroy an entire room; clean-agent suppression is the appropriate choice for most data center spaces.

Today's goal: Explain how HVAC, fire suppression, and power systems protect physical infrastructure and describe the alert thresholds that define safe operating ranges. Handout: pre-lecture-handout.docx.
