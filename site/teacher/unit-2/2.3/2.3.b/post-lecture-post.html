The big picture: The analog-versus-IP tradeoff in this skill is a small, concrete example of a huge pattern in modern security: every time you add intelligence and connectivity to a device, you also add an attack surface that didn't exist before. An analog coaxial camera can be physically cut but can't be hacked over the internet; an IP camera can be watched from anywhere in the world by its owner, and, if the default password was never changed, by anyone else too. That tradeoff (capability versus attack surface) isn't unique to cameras. You'll meet it again with every "smart" device in this course, and the right response is never "don't use the smart version," it's "secure the smart version as seriously as you'd secure a server, because that's what it now is."

Key terms to lock in: - CCTV (Closed-Circuit Television), A video system where signals are transmitted privately to a specific set of monitors and recording units. - IP Camera, A digital video camera that sends and receives data over an IP network. - Active Monitoring, The real-time viewing of live video feeds by security personnel to detect and respond to incidents as they occur. - Passive Monitoring, Recording video feeds for storage and forensic retrieval after an incident. - Blind Spot, An unmonitored area within a facility's layout where an intruder can avoid camera coverage. - Thermal Imaging, A camera technology that detects infrared radiation (heat) rather than visible light, allowing detection in complete darkness or harsh weather.

Common misconceptions -- don't fall for these: - "CCTV prevents physical security breaches.", CCTV is fundamentally a detective control, not a preventive one. A camera cannot stop an intruder from climbing a fence; it only records the action or alerts a guard. To prevent breaches, cameras must be paired with preventive controls (like fences and locks) and active response protocols. - "Networked IP cameras are inherently more secure than old analog systems.", While IP cameras provide better image quality and analytics, they also introduce a cyber-attack surface. An analog coaxial system cannot be hacked over the internet; an IP camera connected to an insecure network can.
