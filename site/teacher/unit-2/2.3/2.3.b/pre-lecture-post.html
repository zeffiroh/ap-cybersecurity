Unit 2 · Securing Spaces · 2.3 Physical Access Controls

Before class today. If locks, badges, and biometrics control who enters a space, surveillance systems tell us what happens in and around that space. Surveillance is a dual-use control. It functions as a detective control by recording events and alerting operators to incidents in progress.

The intuition to hold onto: The framing to hold onto: a camera is a detective control wearing a preventive control's reputation. People install CCTV and feel safer, but a camera has never once physically stopped anyone from doing anything, it only makes it more likely they'll be identified afterward, and the deterrent effect only works if the attacker believes that matters to them. Keep that distinction sharp, because the AP exam loves testing whether students can tell a preventive control from a detective one, and "we have cameras" is one of the most common wrong answers to "how did you prevent this."

A myth worth busting before we start: "CCTV prevents physical security breaches.", CCTV is fundamentally a detective control, not a preventive one. A camera cannot stop an intruder from climbing a fence; it only records the action or alerts a guard. To prevent breaches, cameras must be paired with preventive controls (like fences and locks) and active response protocols.
