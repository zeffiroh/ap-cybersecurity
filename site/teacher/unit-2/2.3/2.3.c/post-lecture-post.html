The big picture: Notice what a guard can do that nothing else in this unit can: reason. A badge reader logs a swipe; a guard notices that the person swiping doesn't match their badge photo, or that their explanation for being in the building doesn't add up. That's the throughline connecting this skill back to social engineering in Unit 1, technology is consistent and tireless but has no judgment, and humans have judgment but get tired and make exceptions. A mature security posture doesn't pick one over the other; it deliberately pairs them so each one covers the other's blind spot. That pairing principle is worth remembering every time this course asks you to evaluate whether a security plan is "strong, adequate, or weak."

Key terms to lock in: - Defense-in-depth, a security architecture that uses multiple independent control layers so that no single failure is sufficient for an attacker to reach an objective - Personnel control, a security measure involving human actors, including guards, receptionists, escorts, and visitor-log administrators - Tailgating (piggybacking), the practice of following an authorized person through a controlled entry without presenting one's own credential - Mantrap, a physical access control consisting of two interlocking doors that prevent more than one person from entering at a time - Visitor log, a record of all non-employee entrants, including sponsor, arrival time, and departure time, used for access accountability - Escort policy, a facility rule requiring that every visitor be accompanied by an authorized employee at all times - Depth score, a metric counting the number of distinct control categories active in a facility; higher scores indicate stronger layered posture

Common misconceptions -- don't fall for these: - "More guards means more security", Redundant personnel in the same role add cost without closing new gaps; the benefit comes from covering additional categories, not duplicating existing ones. - "Technology replaces guards", Automated controls and human controls cover different failure modes; removing guards to rely solely on electronics eliminates judgment, response, and social-engineering detection. - "A visitor log stops attacks", A log is a deterrent and an audit tool, not a barrier; it is effective as part of a layered program that also includes escort policies and physical access controls.
