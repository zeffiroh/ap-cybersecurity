2.3.a, Physical access controls (locks, badges, biometrics, mantraps)

Before class today. Every firewall rule, encryption key, and intrusion-detection alert becomes irrelevant if an attacker can walk through the front door, plug a USB drive into a server, or tailgate an employee into a restricted corridor. Physical security is not a legacy concern; it is the outermost layer of a layered defense model.

The intuition to hold onto: The four controls in this skill, locks, badges, biometrics, mantraps, aren't a menu where you pick the "best" one. They're a stack, and each layer exists specifically to cover a gap the layer before it can't. A lock works with no power and no network, but leaves no record of who used it. A badge leaves a record and can be revoked instantly, but only proves someone had the credential, not that they're the right person. A biometric proves identity, but can't be swapped out if it's compromised the way a badge can. Ask "what does this layer fail to catch" for every control you meet in this course, because that question is what tells you what the next layer needs to do.

A myth worth busting before we start: "A badge reader is enough; locks are old-fashioned.", Locks provide power-independent fallback. If the badge system loses power or is misconfigured, a lock prevents the door from becoming unsecured.
