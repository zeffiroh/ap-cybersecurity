5.2Linux Permission Controls

  • LO 5.2.A: Explain how two properties of a data item, the sensitivity band an organization has assigned it, and the state it happens to be in at the moment, whether resting on a disk, moving across a link, or open in a running process, together decide which protections apply to it and how strong those protections have to be.
  • LO 5.2.B: Identify the controls that operate through policy and process rather than through code: classification schemes, named data owners, least privilege and separation of duties, periodic access review, acceptable-use and retention rules, and the vetting an organization applies to the software it buys.
  • LO 5.2.C: Decide which access control model, discretionary, mandatory, role-based, or attribute-based, suits a given body of applications and data, given who must decide on access, how often the answer changes, and how much administrative effort the organization can sustain.
  • LO 5.2.D: Put an access decision into effect on a Linux system: set ownership and group, choose a permission mode in octal or symbolic form, apply the special bits where they are warranted, control what new files inherit, and reach for a POSIX access control list when the three-class model cannot express the requirement.
