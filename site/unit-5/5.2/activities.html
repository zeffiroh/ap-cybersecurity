5.2 Activities

Linux Permission Controls · 3

Skill 5.2.a · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Access control models (RBAC, DAC, MAC)

Goal

Students practice identifying which access control model (DAC, MAC, or RBAC) is in play in a scenario, and evaluate whether a specific access request should be allowed. Ties to LO 5.2.A.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each group opens the simulation and works through the access-request queue, agreeing on ALLOW or DENY for each request under the model/policy shown before submitting.
  2. For each request, the group also names which model (DAC, MAC, or RBAC) is being enforced and points to the specific rule that produced the decision (owner grant, label comparison, or role permission).
  3. Groups design one original scenario (a subject, object, and model) and trade with another group to decide ALLOW/DENY.

Collaborate

Assign roles: a "policy owner" states the rule in plain language, an "auditor" verifies the decision against the rule, and a "recorder" writes the justification. Rotate roles each request.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: one request they evaluated + the model in play + the ALLOW/DENY decision and why.

Skill 5.2.b · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Roles, permissions, and least privilege

Goal

Students practice auditing role assignments against job functions and identifying least-privilege violations and candidates for role redesign. Ties to LO 5.2.A, 5.2.B.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each group opens the simulation and reviews the fictional company's role assignments, flagging any user whose permissions exceed their job function before submitting.
  2. For each flagged user, the group writes one sentence naming the specific excess permission and the likely cause (privilege creep from a past project, an overly broad role, etc.).
  3. Groups redesign one over-broad role into two or more narrower roles and explain how the redesign reduces blast radius without blocking anyone's actual job.

Collaborate

Assign roles: an "auditor" identifies the violation, a "role designer" proposes the narrower role split, and a "recorder" documents the before/after. Rotate roles for each flagged user.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: one flagged user + the excess permission + the proposed fix (remove permission or split role).

Skill 5.2.c · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Credential theft prevention and lateral movement

Goal

Students trace a fictional authentication log to identify which account(s) show lateral-movement behavior and propose the access-control practice that would have limited the spread. Ties to LO 5.2.B, 5.2.C.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each group opens the simulation and works through the network graph / authentication log, identifying which account's host-hopping pattern crosses the lateral-movement threshold.
  2. For the flagged account, the group writes two sentences: what behavioral signal gave it away (host count, timing, first-time privileged-tool use), and which single access-control practice (MFA, unique local-admin passwords, network segmentation, least privilege) would have stopped or slowed the chain.
  3. Groups sketch a simple before/after network diagram showing how segmentation would have contained the compromised account to a smaller blast radius.

Collaborate

Assign roles: a "log analyst" scans the authentication events, a "defender" proposes the access-control fix, and a "recorder" documents the diagram and justification. Rotate roles for a second scenario if time allows.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: the account they flagged + the signal that gave it away + the fix they'd recommend.
