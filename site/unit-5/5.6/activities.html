5.6 Activities

Application Threat Auditing · 3

Skill 5.6.a · pairs · 20 min · Offline: yes

Application monitoring and logging

Goal

Pairs read a synthetic application log excerpt, identify which source IP is exhibiting brute-force behavior, and state the exact rule that flags it. Ties to LO 5.6.A.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each pair opens the simulation and works through the log-burst cases, deciding ALERT or OK for each source IP based on the >=5 failed logins / 60-second-window rule.
  2. For one ALERT case, the pair writes the exact evidence: how many 401s, over what time span, from which IP.
  3. The pair proposes one additional log field or signal (e.g. distinct usernames targeted, user agent string) that would make the alert even more confident, and explains why.

Collaborate

Assign roles: a "log analyst" scans the burst for the qualifying window, and a "SIEM engineer" states the alert rule in plain English and checks the analyst's math. Swap roles for the next case.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each pair submits: the IP they flagged, the count/window evidence, and the one extra signal they'd add to strengthen the alert.

Skill 5.6.b · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Data loss detection (DLP)

Goal

Groups triage a batch of outbound transfer events, decide which are exfiltration alerts under a fixed DLP rule, and design an approved-destination list that would reduce false positives without missing real threats. Ties to LO 5.6.B.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each group works through the the simulation egress queue, deciding ALERT or OK for each transfer against the >100 MB / non-approved-destination rule.
  2. The group lists every destination that appeared across all cases and sorts them into "should be on the approved list" vs. "should stay unapproved," with a one-sentence justification for each (is it an internal system, a known partner, or an unknown host?).
  3. Groups discuss: could an attacker deliberately keep transfers just under the volume threshold to avoid detection ("low and slow" exfiltration)? What additional signal (time of day, account baseline, destination reputation) would catch that?

Collaborate

Assign roles: an "analyst" applies the fixed rule to each case, a "policy owner" curates the approved-destination list, and a "red teamer" argues how an attacker would evade the rule as written. Debate the red teamer's evasion case as a group.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each group submits: their final approved-destination list, and the one evasion technique the red teamer proposed plus a mitigation for it.

Skill 5.6.c · small group (3-4) · 25 min · Offline: yes

Digital evidence analysis and attack classification

Goal

Groups review evidence bundles, classify each incident, and practice writing a short, honest incident summary that states the classification and any remaining uncertainty. Ties to LO 5.6.B, 5.6.C.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each group works through the the simulation evidence-triage cases (or a printed handout of the same bundles), classifying each as brute-force, SQL injection, malware, or phishing.
  2. For one case, the group writes a two-sentence incident summary in the style a SOC analyst would submit: the classification, the specific evidence that supports it, and (if applicable) any indicator that doesn't fully fit and should be flagged as open uncertainty.
  3. Groups discuss chain-of-custody: for the evidence in their case, what would the group need to document (who collected it, how it was stored) for the classification to hold up under review?

Collaborate

Assign roles: a "junior analyst" collects and lists the evidence, a "senior analyst" challenges the classification and asks "what indicator would change your mind?", and a "reporter" writes the final incident summary combining both perspectives.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each group submits: their classification, the strongest piece of supporting evidence, and one sentence naming any remaining uncertainty (or "none" if the evidence was conclusive).
