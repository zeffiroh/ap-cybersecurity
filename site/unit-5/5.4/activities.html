5.4 Activities

Asymmetric Cryptography · 4

Skill 5.4.a · pairs · 25 min · Offline: yes

Public/private key pairs

Goal

Working with a partner, students hand-compute a small-number RSA key pair from two primes, then correctly classify which key (public or private) is used for four common operations (send-encrypted, receive-decrypt, sign, verify). Ties to LO 5.4.A.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each pair is assigned two small primes (e.g. p = 5, q = 11). By hand, compute n = p * q and phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1).
  2. The pair is given a candidate exponent e (e.g. e = 3) and must verify gcd(e, phi(n)) = 1, then search small integers d until they find one where (e * d) mod phi(n) = 1. Record the (e, n) public key and (d, n) private key.
  3. The pair works through four short scenario cards: "Send Bob a secret message," "Bob reads the message you sent," "Sign a document as Bob," "Verify Bob's signature." For each, they write down whether the public or private key is used and by whom (sender/receiver/signer/verifier).
  4. Pairs open the simulation to check their hand-computed key pair and scenario answers against the simulation's self-check, and try the escalating scenario rounds until they reach the target score.

Collaborate

One partner is the "key generator" (does the n / phi(n) / d arithmetic and narrates each step aloud), the other is the "scenario analyst" (reasons through the four operation cards). Swap roles for a second prime pair if time allows, and compare answers with a neighboring pair.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: their pair's (n, phi(n)) values, the (e, d) pair they found, and one sentence explaining why encrypting with the private key would NOT keep a message confidential.

Skill 5.4.b · pairs · 30 min · Offline: yes

RSA algorithm

Goal

Working with a partner, students hand-compute a complete RSA round trip from two small primes through key derivation, encryption, and decryption, confirming by hand that decrypt(encrypt(m)) == m. Ties to LO 5.4.A, 5.4.B.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each pair is assigned two small primes (e.g. p = 5, q = 11) and computes n = p * q and phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1) by hand.
  2. The pair is given a candidate public exponent e (e.g. e = 3) and must verify gcd(e, phi(n)) = 1, then find d by testing small integers until (e * d) mod phi(n) = 1. This is the modular inverse, the same computation mod_inverse() performs in problem/.
  3. The pair picks a message m less than n (e.g. m = 9) and computes the ciphertext c = m^e mod n by hand (repeated multiplication and reduction mod n, showing intermediate steps rather than jumping straight to a calculator answer).
  4. The pair decrypts: m' = c^d mod n, and confirms m' equals the original message m. If it does not match, they re-check phi(n), d, or the exponentiation arithmetic before moving on.
  5. Pairs open the simulation to check their hand-computed key pair, ciphertext, and decrypted message against the simulation, and try the escalating rounds until they reach the target score.

Collaborate

One partner is the "key deriver" (computes n, phi(n), and finds d, narrating the modular-inverse search aloud), the other is the "cipher operator" (performs the encrypt and decrypt exponentiation steps). Swap roles for a second prime pair if time allows, and compare final (n, e, d, c) values with a neighboring pair working the same primes to confirm they agree.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: their pair's (n, phi(n), e, d) values, the message m and ciphertext c they computed, and one sentence explaining what part of the process an attacker who only has (e, n) and c would be unable to redo without the private key.

Skill 5.4.c · pairs · 25 min · Offline: yes

Digital signatures

Goal

Working with a partner, students hand-trace the hash-then-sign construction for a small RSA key pair, then classify a set of message/signature/public-key combinations as VALID or FORGED/TAMPERED, explaining which of authenticity, integrity, or non-repudiation is violated in each forged case. Ties to LO 5.4.B.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each pair is given the same small key pair used in problem/: p = 61, q = 53 so n = 3233, public exponent e = 17, private exponent d = 2753. Confirm together that this is a signing key pair (private d signs, public e verifies), the reverse roles from confidentiality (5.4.a).
  2. The pair is given a short message (e.g. "pay Bob $50"). Working by hand (or with the simulation as a calculator), they compute digest = SHA-256(message) mod n, then signature = digest^d mod n, writing down each intermediate value.
  3. To verify, the pair recomputes digest from the message independently, then computes recovered = signature^e mod n, and confirms digest == recovered.
  4. The pair opens the simulation and works through its set of message/signature/public-key cases, classifying each as VALID or FORGED/TAMPERED, including a case where the message was edited after signing, and a case where the wrong public key was used to verify. For each FORGED case, they write one sentence naming which guarantee (authenticity, integrity, or non-repudiation) the forgery would have broken if verification had wrongly said VALID.

Collaborate

One partner is the "signer" (computes the digest and signature, narrates each modular exponentiation step aloud), the other is the "verifier" (recomputes the digest independently and checks it against the recovered value). Swap roles for the second case in the simulation and compare reasoning with a neighboring pair on the trickiest forged case.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: the (digest, signature) pair they hand-computed, their VALID/FORGED classification for the simulation's "wrong public key" case, and one sentence explaining why that case is forged even though the signature itself was never altered.

Skill 5.4.d · pairs · 25 min · Offline: yes

PKI and digital certificates

Goal

Working with a partner, students trace five printed certificate-chain scenario cards by hand, decide TRUST or REJECT for each, and write down the specific reason (expired / revoked / broken chain link / untrusted root / bad signature / valid). Ties to LO 5.4.B, 5.4.C.

Materials

Steps

Check off all materials above to unlock the steps.
  1. Each pair receives the trust-store card and five scenario cards, each showing a leaf-first certificate chain (subject, issuer, public key, validity window, revoked flag, and whether the signature verifies, pre-computed and shown as true/false so the exercise stays focused on the chain logic, not the arithmetic).
  2. For each card, the pair walks the chain leaf to root and checks, in order: (a) is the current time inside the validity window, (b) is the certificate revoked, (c) does the issuer field match the next certificate's subject (or, for the top cert, is it in the trust store with a matching public key), (d) does the signature verify. The first check that fails determines the reason; if all pass, the chain is TRUST.
  3. The pair records, for each card: TRUST or REJECT, and if REJECT, which single check failed first.
  4. Pairs open the simulation to check their five answers against the simulation's six scenarios (one extra) and read the explanation for any they got wrong.

Collaborate

One partner is the "chain walker" (narrates the leaf-to-root walk aloud, cert by cert), the other is the "trust store clerk" (checks every top-of-chain subject/key against the trust store card and calls out any mismatch). Swap roles halfway through the five cards.

Exit Ticket

AP

Certificate of Completion

Each student submits: one scenario card's full reasoning chain in their own words, and one sentence explaining why a self-signed certificate with a perfectly valid signature can still be correctly rejected.
